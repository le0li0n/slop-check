# Man and Superman

Shaw's play is remembered for a line most people misattribute to optimism: "The reasonable man adapts himself to the world; the unreasonable one persists in trying to adapt the world to himself. Therefore all progress depends on the unreasonable man."

Venture capital has spent two decades treating this as a due diligence criterion. Unreasonableness became a proxy for conviction, conviction a proxy for outcome. We funded the founder who wouldn't take no, and told ourselves the ones who flamed out were merely insufficiently unreasonable.

It's worth remembering that Shaw put the line in an appendix, in the mouth of a character he wrote as a bit of a fool. The play itself is more interesting than the epigram, and more useful.

## What the play actually argues

*Man and Superman* is about the Life Force — Shaw's notion that evolution has a direction and works through people who are largely unaware they're serving it. Ann Whitefield, the play's real engine, pursues John Tanner with total single-mindedness while Tanner delivers speeches about freedom. He thinks he's the protagonist. He's the instrument.

The uncomfortable read for our industry: the unreasonable founder is not the cause of progress. He is the mechanism through which a market that was going to happen anyway expresses itself. Timing, distribution shifts, cost curves, regulatory windows — these are the Life Force. The founder is Tanner, giving interviews about vision while being carried.

This is not a nihilistic point. It's an allocation point.

## The investing implication

If unreasonableness is a trait, you screen for it in people. If it's a residue of correct conditions, you screen for conditions and then ask which person the conditions require.

Most of the great outcomes in any vintage share a structural feature that was visible ex ante and a personality feature that was not. The structural feature is boring: a cost that fell 90%, a behavior that changed, a rule that lapsed. The personality feature is the story we tell afterward, because it's better narrative and because it flatters the belief that we picked rather than caught.

The practical test is simple. When a founder tells you why now, do you hear a description of the world or a description of themselves? Tanner's speeches are about Tanner. Ann never explains herself once.

## For founders

Shaw's superman was never a great individual. It was a species-level upgrade — the thing that comes after us, produced by us, indifferent to any of our particular ambitions. Being the vehicle for something larger than your own preferences is not a diminishment. It's the only version of the job that survives contact with a market.

The unreasonable man persists in adapting the world to himself. Sometimes the world was already moving, and he had the good sense to persist in its direction.
